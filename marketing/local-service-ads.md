# Local Service Ads (LSA) Setup Runbook

**What LSAs are**: Google Local Service Ads are pay-per-lead ads that appear *above* the Map Pack and PPC results for local-intent queries. They display a "Google Guaranteed" badge.

**Why this matters for ProWood**:
- Average LSA lead cost in cabinet/remodeling: $25–$70/lead
- Compare to PPC: $5–$11/click × ~10 clicks to get 1 lead = $50–$110/lead, and that's *if* the lead form converts
- LSAs are billed per lead, not per click — wasted impressions cost nothing
- For a $500–$1,000/month budget, LSAs typically outperform PPC on cost-per-acquired-customer

**Time investment**: 4–6 hours for setup, then ~30 minutes/week ongoing.

---

## Phase 1: Eligibility check (Day 1)

Before doing the paperwork, confirm ProWood qualifies. LSAs require:

1. **Licensed business in good standing** — Missouri general contractor or trade-specific licensing
2. **Business insurance** — General liability ($1M+ typical) and workers' comp
3. **Background check** — On the business owner (Dave) and any employee who interacts with clients
4. **Service-category eligibility** — Cabinet making, cabinet refacing, and kitchen remodeling are all currently supported LSA categories in the US

Run the eligibility check at [ads.google.com/local-services-ads](https://ads.google.com/local-services-ads). It's a 5-minute form.

## Phase 2: Application (Days 1–7)

### 2.1 Create the LSA account
1. Sign into the same Google account that owns the Google Business Profile (do not create a new one)
2. Start the LSA application
3. Service area: **St. Louis, MO metro** — narrow to Clayton, Chesterfield, Wildwood, and surrounding areas (same as GBP service area for consistency)
4. Job categories to enable (in priority order):
   - **Cabinet maker / cabinet refacing** (primary)
   - **Kitchen designer / kitchen remodeler** (secondary)
   - **Carpenter** (catch-all backup)

### 2.2 Upload documentation
Have these scanned/photographed and ready:
- Missouri business license / registration
- General liability insurance certificate (download from your carrier)
- Workers' comp certificate
- Driver's license (Dave + any other employees doing client visits)
- Business EIN / W-9 ready

### 2.3 Background check ($45)
- Initiated through Pinkerton (Google's vendor)
- Dave + every employee on site at client homes must pass
- Takes 5–14 business days for Pinkerton + Google review

### 2.4 Total approval timeline
Realistically: **2–4 weeks** from application to live ads. The background check is the slowest step.

## Phase 3: Budget and bidding (Day of activation)

### 3.1 Weekly budget
Start at **$150–$250/week** ($600–$1,000/month). Adjust after 30 days based on lead quality.

**Math**: At $40 avg/lead, $200/week = 5 leads/week = 20 leads/month. Closing rate for refacing leads is typically 10–25% (industry data) — so 2–5 closed projects/month is a reasonable target. At $25K avg project value, 1 closed lead/month already pays back.

### 3.2 Bidding strategy
LSAs use a different bidding model than PPC. Choose:

- **Maximize Leads** (recommended for first 60 days) — Google's auto-bidding, optimized for volume
- **Maximize Job Type** — once you have data on which job types convert best, switch to this and bid more aggressively on cabinet refacing leads

### 3.3 Lead types
You'll be charged for:
- Phone calls lasting >30 seconds
- Message leads (Google's contact form)
- Booking leads (if you've set up booking through the LSA dashboard)

You will **not** be charged for:
- Sales calls / spam
- Wrong number / disconnected calls
- Customers outside service area (if Google detects it)

If a charged lead is bad, **dispute it within 30 days** through the LSA dashboard — Google credits about 60–70% of disputed leads in our experience for similar industries.

## Phase 4: Profile optimization (Day of activation)

Your LSA profile reuses GBP data but adds a few LSA-specific fields. Optimize:

### 4.1 Photo strategy (3–5 hero photos)
LSAs show a small set of photos prominently. Pick the **best 5 project photos** from your gallery:
- 2 cabinet refacing before/after
- 1 custom kitchen
- 1 built-in
- 1 shop / team shot (humanizes)

### 4.2 Highlights
LSAs let you add short "Highlights" — pick 3–5:
- "Family-owned since 1985"
- "40+ years of St. Louis kitchens"
- "Free in-home consultation"
- "Solid-wood construction"
- "Local shop, not a franchise"

### 4.3 Business description
Use the same one from GBP — but cut to ~500 characters if needed.

### 4.4 Response time
Critical: **respond to every LSA lead within 1 hour** during business hours. Google factors response time into the ad rank. Set up phone-call routing so Dave's mobile rings on LSA leads.

## Phase 5: Lead handling SOP (ongoing)

LSAs deliver leads to your phone/email. Your workflow:

### 5.1 Phone leads
1. Answer within 3 rings if possible (Google tracks)
2. Qualify in first 2 minutes:
   - Service type (refacing vs. full remodel)
   - City (in Clayton/Chesterfield/Wildwood? other?)
   - Approximate timeline
   - Budget realism (is this a $5K project they want or $30K?)
3. Book in-home consultation if qualified
4. If not a fit (out of area, wrong service, unrealistic budget) — be polite, redirect, **dispute the lead** in the LSA dashboard

### 5.2 Message leads
Reply within 1 hour, **always** — even if it's "Thanks for reaching out, can I call you in 15 minutes?" Google measures response speed.

### 5.3 Track leads in a simple spreadsheet
Columns:
- Date received
- Lead type (call/message)
- Source (LSA, GBP, PPC, organic)
- City
- Service
- Qualified? (Y/N)
- Consultation booked? (Y/N)
- Closed? (Y/N, project value)
- Lead cost (LSA charge or PPC cost)

This is how you'll know which channels are actually profitable. Review monthly.

## Phase 6: Tracking and optimization (Ongoing)

### Weekly (15 minutes)
- Dispute any bad leads from the past 7 days
- Respond to any unanswered messages
- Quick scan of "Job types" performance — are kitchen remodel leads better than refacing leads, or vice versa? Adjust bid distribution.

### Monthly (30 minutes)
- Review lead-to-consultation conversion rate
- Review consultation-to-close rate
- Calculate cost per closed customer (`monthly LSA spend ÷ projects closed`)
- Adjust budget up or down based on closed-project ROI

### Quarterly (1 hour)
- Refresh photos (rotate in newer projects)
- Update highlights based on what's resonating
- Audit service area boundaries

---

## Common LSA mistakes to avoid

1. **Not disputing bad leads** — Disputing protects your budget. Most contractors leave money on the table here.
2. **Slow response times** — A lead unanswered for 4 hours has half the close rate of one answered in 30 minutes.
3. **Treating LSA leads like PPC leads** — LSA leads are typically higher intent (they clicked through a Google-vetted ad). Approach with that mindset.
4. **Over-bidding for kitchen remodel** — Refacing leads are usually cheaper *and* easier to close for ProWood. Bias your bid distribution toward refacing.
5. **Forgetting that the GBP work feeds this** — LSA rank is heavily influenced by GBP review count and quality. The two systems are linked.
